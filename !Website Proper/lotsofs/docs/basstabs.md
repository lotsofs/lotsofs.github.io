# The basstabs module

Imported by `CLAUDE.md`.

Added 2026-10-23. The owner's shorthand sheets for 5-string bass in standard tuning (B E A D G): enough to remember a song by, with root-note frets and only as much rhythm as the notation marks. No accounts, no database, no i18n, no JS. It is public, since anyone with the address can read it and the owner reads it on a phone, but it does not ship for now: it is in `$unshippedModules` (TAB). Every page is `noindex` by the owner's choice, through the meta tag in `views/partials/head.php`, so a sheet can be reached by link but stays out of search results. Never block these paths in a `robots.txt` as well: a crawler that may not fetch a page never sees its tag.

- **A song is one file**, `app/modules/basstabs/songs/<slug>.php`, returning `['title', 'artist', 'sections' => [['name', 'notes'], ...]]`. `'key' => 'C minor'` is the song's key, printed with the artist in smaller grey type in the page's top-left corner, across from the title (`The Cardigans · C minor`), either one alone when the other is missing. `'accidentals' => 'flat'` names the black keys in flats (B♭) rather than the default sharps (A♯); pick whichever the song's key is written in. A section may also carry `'text'`, a line printed under its name for what the notes alone do not say (the outro's "riff 2x, then end of riff 2x"), and `'board' => ['key' => 'C minor', 'anchor' => 'E8', 'position' => 'E8 E10 E11 A8 A10 A11 D8 D10']`, a fretboard drawn beside it (below). A board takes `'accidentals'` of its own too, for a chord that reads wrong in the song's spelling: Creep's C minor in flats on a sheet otherwise in sharps. A section may also be only a board, with no `notes` and no `name`: Love Myself's four chord boards are written that way. A run of such sections is one block, the boards one above another at full size, left-aligned, except under a section with a board beside it, where they right-align (`.bassTabBoardsRight`, decided in `bassTabBlocks()` in `tabs.php`) to stand in that board's column: Inside's C and A major under its verse's E minor. Boards in a block are 4 px apart, frame to frame, and a right-aligned block sits 4 px under the board above it; Inside fills 941 of the 1009 px a page holds that way (2026-10-26); a printed page may break between two of them but never through one. Love Myself's riff and four boards fill 809 of the 1009 px an A4 page holds (2026-10-26). A section may carry `'lines' => [...]` too, printed as a plain list after its staff, or as the whole section with no name, as Monday Morning's run of lyric cues is; such a section's missing heading is also why no bracket band rises into it. A section with no notes, board or lines is refused. `'beside' => true` puts a section on the same line as the one before it, under a heading of its own, as Creep's outro is beside its main riff: `bassTabBlocks()` gathers them into a row (`.bassTabRow`, a wrapping flexbox), each section as wide as its staff and the last taking the rest of the line, so its staff's lines run on to the page edge like any section's; on a phone the later ones wrap under. Neither may have a board, whose column needs the section's full width, and `beside` on the first section or after a block of boards is refused. A row takes no part in the bleed below: its later headings stand further right, over labels the route cannot see. The file name is the address. `routes.php` globs that folder and registers `/basstabs/<slug>` for each file, all to `routes/song.php`, which reads the slug back with `getLastUrlPart()`. The router matches whole paths only, so this is how there is one page per song without a query string, and it is why a slug can only reach the `require` in `bassTabSong()` when a file by that name exists. The name must be lowercase kebab case: the router 301s any other path to its lowercase, which has no route. A test checks every file name.
- **Adding a song** (the usual request: a photo of a handwritten sheet, or a list of notes) is one new file in `songs/` and a run of the suite. Nothing is registered anywhere else. Spell the title the way the record does.
- **The notation is one string per section**, split on whitespace except inside a quoted label, and parsed by `bassTabParse()` in `tabs.php`. My Favourite Game's chorus uses all of it.
  - `E8` is a note: a string letter (`G D A E B`, uppercase) and a fret from 0 to 24 with no leading zero. `E8-` is held: twice as long, drawn with a thick line out of it to the right, and each further dash doubles it again (`E8--` four times, `E8---` eight, no more). `B8/2` is half as long, and a `/2` after dashes halves them: Amsterdam's quiet part, `B4--/2 E3-/2 E1-/2`, keeps its holds and their proportions in half the room. Held is the line, not the length (`held` on the parsed note), so `E3-/2` is drawn held while lasting no longer than a plain note. After the length comes at most one mark drawn over the digit: `:` two dots (Amsterdam's 8th notes), `.` one dot (Inside's riff and verse, its meaning the owner's) or `~` a tilde (Amsterdam's bridge's "played differently"). Held was `~` until the owner wanted the tilde for the mark. `B8^"Drum fill"` puts an arrow under the note with that label, last of all (`E8-:^"x"`). A mark over a note on the top string moves the staff's lines down 10 px, so the mark is not cut off above.
  - `|` is a bar line, `:|` one that ends a repeat (dots before it), `|:` one that starts one. The owner's sheets mark a repeated block with `:|` alone.
  - `h` on its own between two notes on one string, the second higher, is a hammer-on (the owner writes `5-7`): an arc from digit to digit with a small `h` over it, which breaks the line above the way a digit does. It takes no room of its own. A hammer-on over the top string moves the lines down 16 px, as a mark does 10.
  - `s` on its own between two notes on one string, on different frets, is a slide (the owner writes `5/7`; `/` itself was taken by the line break): a short slash just before the second digit, rising for a slide up and falling for one down (`data-slide`). It is placed from that digit, so after a held note it rises out of the end of the hold, and takes no room of its own.
  - `/` on its own starts a new line within the section: everything after it is a staff of its own, under the same heading, and `bassTabStaves()` lays each out with `bassTabLayout()`. Brackets cannot cross one. Break at a bar line or one of the page's own note groups, and keep every line within the 680 px of an A4 column, so the sheet prints at full size; one wide line shrinks every staff on the sheet, through the shared factor below. Bring Me to Life was written with them throughout; the owner has since taken some out, and its chorus no longer fits the column.
  - `r` on its own is a rest a beat long, drawn as a quarter-rest zigzag through the middle line; `r/2` is an eighth rest, half a beat, drawn as a dot, a flick and a slanted stem, which is the 7 the owner writes for one. A rest takes room like a note and counts as one for bar lines and brackets, but has no note name, and a hammer-on beside one is refused.
  - `,` on its own adds a small gap that means nothing musically: it groups notes for the eye, as between the bridge's `E8 E9` pairs.
  - `["Only 2nd chorus"` … `]` draws a bracket under everything between, with the label under it. The label is optional and brackets do not nest. A bracket end that meets a bar line, inside the bracket or just outside it, stops on that bar line, so `:| ["x" E8 … :| ]` spans bar line to bar line.
  - Anything else throws `InvalidArgumentException` naming the token, so a typo fails the suite, which requests every sheet, instead of drawing a wrong one. Extend this same string rather than adding a second shape beside it.
- **The staff is inline SVG**, laid out by `bassTabLayout()` (all the geometry, in px) and printed by `views/partials/staff.php`. Five lines, top to bottom G D A E B (`BASS_TAB_STRINGS`), so the low B is the bottom line as on the owner's paper sheets. The gap after a note is its length (30 px for an ordinary one), plus 10 px for a `,`, so spacing carries what the notation marks and nothing more. A bar line adds 16 px and sits midway between its neighbours, measured from the end of a held note's line rather than its digit. Each fret is a `<text>` with a thick stroke in the paper colour painted under the glyph (`paint-order: stroke`), which breaks the line behind the digit as handwritten tab does. Lines and frets carry `data-string`, which is what the tests read sections back from. Under the bottom line is a row of note names, one under each fret, worked out by `bassTabNoteName()` from each open string's pitch (`BASS_TAB_OPEN_PITCHES`) plus the fret; brackets and arrows come below that row, so an arrow points up through the name at its note. Label widths are guessed, there being no font metrics on the server.
- **On a narrow screen every staff shrinks, by one factor per sheet.** Each SVG has a `viewBox` and the width `min(<its px>, <its share of the sheet's widest staff>cqw)`, so the widest fills the sheet and the rest shrink with it, which keeps digits the same size from section to section. The unit is `cqw` against `.bassTabSheet`, a size container, rather than `%`, because a staff beside a fretboard sits in a narrower column and a percentage of that column would shrink it out of step with the others. At full size nothing changes. The lines run to x=5000, far past the `viewBox`, and the SVG is `overflow: visible` inside an `overflow: hidden` box: that is what still carries them to the edge of the page. One long section shrinks everything on its sheet: My Favourite Game's bridge, at 614 px, takes the whole sheet to about 0.58 on a 390 px phone, where the note names come out around 7 px. Wrapping a section onto a second staff is the fix if that proves too small.
- **A section with a `board` gets a fretboard laid on top of its staff's right end**, as if slapped on. The section is a two-column grid (`.bassTabWithBoard`): the staff column is at least the staff's own width and takes what is left, the board column at most the board's width and gives way first, the board scaling down inside it; below a 560 px sheet a container query stacks the board under the staff. The staff's lines are not cut at their column: its clip box is `overflow: visible` there, and the section's own `overflow: hidden` stops them at the page edge instead, so they run across the gap and on under the board, which is positioned above them and hides them behind its filled frame. The board is 482 px wide plus its key column, frame included (35 px fret cells, 6 px inside the frame), sized so it fits beside a short staff on an A4 page, which is where the owner wants them: in the space to the right of a riff. `bassTabFretboard()` lays it out and `views/partials/fretboard.php` prints it: the five strings as on the staff, the open string left of a thick nut, frets 1 to 12 (`BASS_TAB_BOARD_FRETS`), and a named dot on every fret whose pitch is in the key. The cells of the frets a bass marks with a dot (`BASS_TAB_BOARD_INLAYS`: 3, 5, 7, 9 and 12) are shaded a faint off-white. **Greys only**, because the owner prints in greyscale, and a test holds every `--board*` colour to a grey: in the key is light with an ink name, the hand position mid grey and its anchor near black, both with white names. The notes of the key's own triad, its first, third and fifth (`bassTabFretboard()` takes them off the scale, so a Phrygian board gets its minor triad), carry a heavier border: 3 px ink in the hand position, a lighter 2.5 px dark grey (`--boardChord`) outside it, which the owner settled on after 3 px ink everywhere was too much and 2 px mid grey too little (`.bassTabBoardChord`) (the mid grey is dark enough, #707070, for white to read). There is no legend, by the owner's choice. The frame is the SVG's first element, a rounded rect filled with the paper colour and stroked in `--boardOutline`, and the key's name stands inside it in a column left of the string letters, root over scale (`G`, then `major` level with the D string). It headed the board until 2026-10-26, when the owner moved it to win back 20 px a board, which took Creep's four from 1031 px, a second page, to 958. The column is `bassTabKeyColumn()`'s guess at 8.5 px a character of the longest word among every board's key on the sheet, plus 8, so a stack of boards keeps its nuts in line; measured in Chrome, every key name clears the string letters by 8 to 12 px (2026-10-26). A board beside a staff shrinks a little for the extra width. It is drawn in the SVG rather than as a CSS border and background because a print leaves CSS backgrounds out, and without the fill the staff's lines would show through the board on paper. `bassTabKey()` reads a root (`A`–`G`, then `b`, `#`, `♭` or `♯` if any) and a scale from `BASS_TAB_SCALES`: `major`, `minor` (natural minor) or `Phrygian` (Amsterdam's D Phrygian). The scale's name is matched in any case, and `bassTabKeyName()` prints a key the one way, with real ♭ and ♯ signs and the scale spelt as the constant spells it, so `Bb phrygian` comes out `B♭ Phrygian`. The song's key and every board's go through it. The position is written in the tab notation and must be plain notes, in the key and on the board, with the anchor one of them; anything else throws, so a typo fails the suite like a bad note does. The board shrinks by itself rather than by the staves' shared factor, since it is not a staff; its strings are `bassTabBoardString`, not `bassTabLine`, so a test reading a section's staff lines never picks up the board's. A section with a board is `break-inside: avoid`, so a print never splits it. Amsterdam has one, D Phrygian beside the quiet part, anchored on A5 with A5 A6 A8, D5 D7 D8, G5 G7; it prints at about 91% (2026-10-26), because the section's long heading holds the staff column wider than its staff. My Favourite Game has two: C minor beside the riff, anchored on E8 with the minor box at frets 8–11 (E8 E10 E11, A8 A10 A11, D8 D10), and F major beside the outro, anchored on B6, the riff's F, with the major shape at frets 5–8 (B6 B8, E5 E6 E8, A5 A7 A8). Its boards print at 95% and 92%, and the sheet fills 685 of the 1009 px an A4 page holds (2026-10-26).
- **A sheet longer than a page breaks between sections.** Every section is `break-inside: avoid`, every staff line too, and a heading is `break-after: avoid`, so a section moves whole to the next page instead of being split or leaving its heading behind. Sections are 0.75em apart, and a staff with a bracket or arrow keeps only 30 px under its note names (`$under`), the bracket 6 px below the name row and its label 22: both were trimmed to close the gap the owner found too big under a bracketed staff.
- **That band may rise beside the next heading**, which is what got Bring Me to Life onto one page (941 of 1009 px, 2026-10-23). The section's last staff keeps its full height but takes a negative bottom margin of the band's height (`.bassTabBleed`, `--bleed`), scaled with the staff through the same `cqw` share as its width, so the next heading moves up level with the bracket's label. `routes/song.php` allows it only where nothing can collide: the next block is a section, neither section has a board (a board's top edge is level with its heading), and every label in the band starts right of the heading's text, estimated at 11 px a character of 1.1rem bold against each staff's `labelLeft`. Only a section's last line, so a band never rises into its own section's next staff, and never below a 560 px sheet, where staves shrink and headings do not.
- **One look on every screen and in print: paper.** There is no dark theme, on purpose. `@page` is A4 with 15 mm margins, and the content column is at most 180 mm, the width between them. The title sits top right in large letters so it shows while leafing through a stack of prints, the artist and key in the top-left corner (`.bassTabCorner`), which costs the page no height of its own. The "All songs" link is hidden in print.
